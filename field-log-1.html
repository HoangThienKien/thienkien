Field Log · Roads & Journeys · 2024

Before the event,
there was the road.

A motorcycle journey northward from Huế after Tết. Every stop, every person, every bowl of soup — preparing something that no logistics sheet could prepare.

Post-Tết 2024 Huế → Hà Nội → Sơn La ~1,800 words Hoàng Đại

There are journeys that do not begin at airports or bus stations. This one began with a quiet agreement between Hải Ninh and me — that no matter how busy work became, we would always make time once a year to get on a motorbike and leave for a while.

Not as tourists.
Not entirely for work either.

Just to move. To observe. To meet people again. And to remind ourselves that Vietnam is still much larger than whatever can fit onto a phone screen.

In early 2024, we chose the stretch between Tết and the return of ordinary routines. People were slowly leaving family gatherings behind and settling back into work. We were doing the opposite.

We started in Huế.
And headed north.

"The deeper purpose of the journey was to prepare for an important event in Hanoi. But the way we prepared was never through spreadsheets or online meetings."

Hoàng Đại — Thiện Kiến

We were preparing a cultural environment for an event hosted by New Energy NEXUS Vietnam in collaboration with the Australian Embassy in Hanoi. The venue itself was designed entirely in a Western style, and our task was to transform it into a space that carried the breath of Vietnam — not through artificial decoration, but through something more genuine.

And the only way to create that kind of authenticity was to live through it ourselves first.

✦

Hoành Sơn Quan (橫山關) — A Historical Threshold

Đèo Ngang · Hà Tĩnh / Quảng Bình

Crossing the Hoành Sơn range from the Quảng Bình side, the verse of Bà Huyện Thanh Quan lingered quietly in our minds: "Bước tới Đèo Ngang bóng xế tà..." The modern highway now runs beneath the mountain pass, but we chose to climb toward the summit instead — where Hoành Sơn Quan still stands.

Standing there, Quảng Bình stretched to the south while Hà Tĩnh lay to the north. It was impossible not to think of the famous words of Trạng Trình Nguyễn Bỉnh Khiêm:

"Hoành Sơn nhất đái, vạn đại dung thân"
“Past Hoành Sơn lies a land where generations may endure.”

A single sentence that would alter the course of Vietnamese history — when Nguyễn Hoàng led his household and followers across this mountain pass, laying the foundation for the southern realm of Đàng Trong.

One prophecy.
One decision.
One frontier.

And history turned in another direction.

At the foot of the pass stood a small folk shrine dedicated to Mother Goddess Liễu Hạnh, who, according to local belief, once appeared there disguised as a roadside water seller. We stopped to offer incense and give thanks for a safe journey.

From there, we continued north through Kỳ Anh, Cẩm Xuyên, and into Hà Tĩnh City — once known as Thành Sen, the Lotus Citadel.

✦

Lam Kinh and the Flavours Along the Way

Thanh Hoá

Past Nghệ Tĩnh, we entered the land of Thanh Hoá and headed straight toward Lam Kinh — the former imperial retreat of the Later Lê dynasty. But the road leading there was just as memorable as the destination itself: Thanh Hoá’s nem chua and Tứ Trụ black sticky rice cakes.

Along both sides of the road, these foods appeared everywhere, laid out as though they were inseparable from the landscape itself — because, in many ways, they were.

“Vietnamese culture reveals itself through the food of each region.
Each place carries its own character.
And at the place where it is made, everything feels entirely different.”

✦

Bánh cáy and hot fresh-green-tea-leaves — Thái Bình

Thái Bình

We passed through Thái Bình without planning to stop. But the bitter cold after Tết wrapped itself around the rider’s hands until it became impossible to keep going any longer.

Hungry and freezing, we pulled over at a small roadside tea stall. A few pieces of Thái Bình’s bánh cáy and a cup of hot tea slowly brought warmth back before the road continued north again.

That was the kind of thing no map could ever point to.
You only understand it once you are sitting there yourself.

✦

Returning to Hanoi

By the time we finally arrived in Hanoi, we were carrying neither products nor ingredients.

What we brought back instead were fragments of a larger picture — Hoành Sơn Quan and its centuries-old prophecy, the scent of Thanh Hoá’s nem chua, the warmth of roadside tea in Thái Bình, and the feeling of having travelled through a country we once thought we understood, only to realise how much more there still was to learn.

The cultural environment we prepared for the event in Hanoi — from the way the space was arranged, to the ingredients we chose, to the stories placed quietly within each detail — all carried traces of this journey.

Not because we had planned it that way.

But because it was the only way we knew how to prepare.

“We did not gather ingredients on this journey.
We gathered understanding instead.”

Thiện Kiến — 2024

The journey continued — through Nam Định, Hải Dương, and Hưng Yên, before eventually leading us into Sơn La, where we met a small family living in the highlands, in a place where children learn to stand without baby walkers.

But perhaps the most important thing this journey reminded us of was this:

To go before preparing — not for research, but to remember why preparation matters in the first place.

Thiện Kiến
